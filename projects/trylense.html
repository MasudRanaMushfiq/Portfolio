TryLense is an online eyewear shop that needed reliable tracking and stronger protection against fake orders while also giving customers a way to preview eyewear before buying.

Problem

A large number of fake orders were being placed, which caused Meta to charge for bad conversions and made the marketing data unreliable. Buyers also needed a better way to test eyewear before checkout.

Solution

I set up Meta Pixel, moved abnormal orders into a fake-order flow, and added blocking logic to prevent repeat bad users from placing multiple fake orders. I also added a virtual try-on flow that can open the selfie camera or use an uploaded image, track the face, show the eyewear on the user, and allow download and manual adjustment controls.

Key Features

  • Online eyewear shop
  • Meta Pixel and conversion tracking
  • Fake-order handling for abnormal purchases
  • Blocklist logic for repeat fraudulent users
  • Virtual try-on with selfie camera or uploaded image
  • Face-tracked preview with download and manual controls

Challenges & Engineering Deep Dive

Face Tracking Across Live Camera and Uploaded Images

The virtual try-on had to work in two modes: live selfie camera input on mobile and laptop, plus uploaded images for users who wanted to test eyewear from a saved photo. The challenge was to keep the eyewear aligned naturally on different face shapes, camera angles, and image sizes without making the experience feel slow or fragile.


How I solved it: I used face tracking to anchor the eyewear position to facial landmarks, then exposed manual adjustment controls so users could fine-tune placement when the automatic fit needed help. That kept the virtual try-on usable across both camera capture and uploaded images.

Fake Orders Without Hurting Real Buyers

The store was getting a high volume of fake orders, and Meta was charging for those bad conversions. The tricky part was reducing fraud without creating friction for genuine customers or hiding useful conversion data.


How I solved it: I separated abnormal orders into a fake-order flow, added blocking logic for repeat bad users, and kept tracking in place with Meta Pixel so the business could still measure real performance while cutting down wasted ad spend.
